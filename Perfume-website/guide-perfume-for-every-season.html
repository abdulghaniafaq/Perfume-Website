Which scent for which season

7 min read By Perfume X Collections Updated November 2026

Quick answer

Choose lighter, fresher perfumes (citrus, aquatic, light woods, usually eau de toilette) for hot and humid weather, and richer ones (amber, gourmand, oud, usually eau de parfum) for cool or cold weather. Heat amplifies scent and cold mutes it, so use fewer sprays in summer and a few more in winter.

Heat, cold and humidity change how a perfume behaves on your skin. The same fragrance can feel crisp in January and suffocating in May. Choosing by season isn't a rule, just a shortcut to what will feel good on you and not overwhelm everyone around you.

Three ideas that make choosing easy

  1. Heat amplifies, cold mutes. In hot weather a perfume projects further and can turn heavy fast, so go lighter in strength and use fewer sprays. In the cold, scent stays closer to the skin, so richer fragrances and a little more spray work well.
  2. Match the family to the weather. Think fresh, airy and watery when it's hot, warm, resinous and sweet when it's cool. The scent families in our catalogue (Fresh & Citrus, Floral, Amber & Spicy, Oud & Woody, Leather & Smoky and Gourmand) line up with this naturally.
  3. Strength matters as much as scent. Eau de toilette (EDT) is the lighter, brighter style. Eau de parfum (EDP) is richer and usually lasts longer. Parfum or extrait is the most concentrated. The usual ranges (about 5–15% fragrance oil for EDT, 15–20% for EDP, 20–30% for parfum) are industry habits, not legal standards, so treat them as a rough guide.

The months are rough. The seasons arrive at different times in different cities, so go by the weather where you live.

Roughly February to March · mild and bright

Spring: soft and floral

Mild days and blossoming gardens suit fragrances that smell like fresh flowers and green leaves. Look in the Floral family: rose, jasmine, peony, magnolia and lily, often with a green or lightly fruity top. An eau de toilette or a light eau de parfum keeps it airy.

Roughly July to September · warm, humid and wet

Monsoon: clean and green

Humid air carries scent well, and many people find very sweet or heavy fragrances cloying in muggy weather. Aim for clean and fresh: citrus, aquatic notes, green and light woody accords. Lighter strengths and “sport” styles tend to stay pleasant when the air is thick.

By day, by night

Season isn't the only thing that matters. For the office, a classroom or a hot afternoon, keep it light, clean and soft. For evenings and occasions, go richer and deeper. A winter-style scent can work in summer at night with a single spray, and a fresh citrus can lift a cold afternoon. Wear what makes you feel good, because these are guidelines, not rules.

Common questions

Which perfume is best for summer in India?

Choose a fresh, light fragrance: citrus, neroli, mint, marine or light aromatic notes, usually an eau de toilette or a light eau de parfum. Heat makes scent travel, so start with one or two sprays. Our Fresh & Citrus family is the place to start.

What perfume should I wear in the monsoon?

Humid air carries scent well, so clean and fresh usually works best: citrus, aquatic notes, green and light woody accords, in a light strength. Many people find very sweet or heavy fragrances cloying in muggy weather, so save those for cooler months.

What is the best perfume for winter?

Cold air mutes scent, so winter suits rich styles: vanilla, tonka, coffee, caramel, oud, sandalwood and resins, usually in eau de parfum or parfum strength. Our Gourmand and Oud & Woody families are the ones to explore, and you can use a few more sprays than in summer.

Does the weather change how a perfume smells?

Yes. Heat amplifies a perfume and makes it project further, while cold keeps it closer to the skin and mutes it. That is why lighter strengths and fewer sprays suit hot weather, and richer strengths suit cold weather.

Is eau de toilette or eau de parfum better for hot weather?

Eau de toilette (EDT) or a light eau de parfum (EDP) usually suits heat, because they are lighter and brighter. The usual ranges (about 5–15% fragrance oil for EDT and 15–20% for EDP) are industry habits, not legal standards, so use them as a rough guide.
